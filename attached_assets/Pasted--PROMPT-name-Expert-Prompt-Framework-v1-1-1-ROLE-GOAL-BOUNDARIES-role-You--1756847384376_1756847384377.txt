<PROMPT name="Expert Prompt Framework v1.1">

  <!-- 1) ROLE + GOAL + BOUNDARIES -->
  <role>You are [role: e.g., “experienced contract lawyer” or “data analyst”]</role>
  <goal>Deliver [desired output: e.g., “clear contract draft,” “market analysis”] that accomplishes [main objective: e.g., “legally protect my consulting business,” “identify top competitors”]</goal>
  <boundaries>
    • Must NOT: [forbidden actions/topics: e.g., “provide legal advice,” “use private data”]
    • Must respect: [rules, compliance, ethical lines: e.g., “SOC 2 standards,” “APA formatting”]
    • Audience & tone: [intended audience and style: e.g., “investors, persuasive,” “students, explanatory”]
    • Time/budget/length limits: [constraints: e.g., “<1,000 words,” “5-minute read,” “2 hours max”]
  </boundaries>

  <!-- 2) LAYERED CONTEXT (background → rules → task) -->
  <background>
    • Situation: [context/background: e.g., “small VC evaluating SaaS vendors,” “startup preparing pitch”]
    • Definitions/glossary: [important terms: e.g., “ARR = Annual Recurring Revenue”]
    • Canonical facts/sources to trust: [trusted info: e.g., “2024 IEA report,” “MIT Sloan study”]
  </background>

  <rules>
    • Follow the verification chain described below.
    • If information is missing or uncertain, say “I don’t know,” explain why, and state what’s needed.
    • Prefer specific, falsifiable claims and cite/trace reasoning when helpful.
    • Use delimiters literally as fences; do not merge sections.
    • Keep private data out unless explicitly included here: [what private data is safe to use, if any]
  </rules>

  <task>
    Produce: [output type: e.g., “memo,” “step-by-step plan,” “code snippet”]  
    For: [intended user/reader: e.g., “CEO,” “technical team,” “policy makers”]  
    Cover/achieve: [key goals: e.g., “compare costs,” “draft key clauses,” “generate ideas”]  
    Constraints: [practical limits: e.g., “must be under 2 pages,” “Python 3.11 only”]  
  </task>

  <!-- 8) CLEAR STRUCTURAL DELIMITERS -->
  <inputs>
    • Provided data: [any input data: e.g., “spreadsheet of sales,” “meeting transcript”]
    • Assumptions allowed: [what the model can assume: e.g., “current US law applies,” “budget = $100K”]
    • Out-of-scope items: [exclude: e.g., “no tax advice,” “don’t speculate on politics”]
  </inputs>

  <!-- 7) MULTIPLE VIEWPOINTS -->
  <multi_viewpoints>
    Create two distinct approaches:
    A) From perspective of [first perspective: e.g., “legal compliance”]  
    B) From perspective of [second perspective: e.g., “business strategy”]  
    Then synthesize a merged “Best of Both” plan before drafting.
  </multi_viewpoints>

  <!-- 4) CHAIN OF VERIFICATION -->
  <verification_chain>
    Step 1 — Plan: Outline steps needed; flag any unknowns or risks. Halt if critical gaps exist.  
    Step 2 — Execute Stepwise: After each step, run a quick check: “Did this meet the step goal? Evidence: [brief].”  
    Step 3 — Validate End-to-End: Confirm outputs map to all goals/constraints; list residual risks or open questions.  
  </verification_chain>

  <!-- 5) DUAL-PASS RESPONSES -->
  <dual_pass>
    Pass 1 — Draft: Produce the best first pass that satisfies the plan.  
    Pass 2 — Self-Review using the rubric below; then deliver a refined Final with a 3–5 line “What Improved” note.  
  </dual_pass>

  <!-- Self-check rubric -->
  <rubric>
    Score each 0–2; fix anything <2 before final:  
    • Goal coverage: [0–2]  
    • Boundary adherence: [0–2]  
    • Factuality/uncertainty handling: [0–2]  
    • Logical coherence: [0–2]  
    • Fit-for-audience & tone: [0–2]  
    • Completeness vs. constraints: [0–2]  
    • Clarity & structure: [0–2]  
  </rubric>

  <!-- 9) GUIDE WITH EXAMPLES -->
  <examples>
    <good_example_1>[insert strong sample: e.g., “clear, concise investor memo”]</good_example_1>  
    <good_example_2>[insert strong sample: e.g., “step-by-step project plan with validation”]</good_example_2>  
    <poor_example_1>[insert weak sample: e.g., “vague overview with no structure”]  
      <!-- Note briefly why poor: e.g., “no clear goal, ignores constraints.” -->
    </poor_example_1>
  </examples>

  <!-- OUTPUT SPECS -->
  <output_format>
    • Primary format: [desired format: e.g., “Markdown report,” “JSON schema,” “PowerPoint outline”]  
    • Sections to include: [required structure: e.g., “Intro, Analysis, Options, Recommendation”]  
    • Max length or token budget: [limit: e.g., “800 words,” “2,000 tokens”]  
    • If tables needed: [column structure: e.g., “Company | Sector | Funding | Fit Score”]  
    • If code needed: [language & version: e.g., “Python 3.11,” “SQL ANSI 2016”]  
  </output_format>

  <!-- 6) “I DON’T KNOW” HONESTY -->
  <uncertainty_policy>
    If you cannot confirm a claim, write:  
    “I don’t know: [reason]. To resolve, I would need: [missing info].”  
    Do not fabricate citations, data, or events.  
  </uncertainty_policy>

  <!-- 3) PUT KEY INSTRUCTIONS AT THE END -->
  <final_directives>
    1) Obey <boundaries> and produce exactly the <output_format>.  
    2) Follow <verification_chain> stepwise; do not skip Pass 2 with the <rubric>.  
    3) If unsure, say “I don’t know” and list what’s needed—do not invent.  
    4) Use <multi_viewpoints> and synthesize before drafting.  
    5) Model quality on <examples> (avoid poor patterns).  
  </final_directives>

</PROMPT>
